Section 1

Why this document exists

1.1

The Method needs worked examples. A prospect who has never met us will not hand over a general ledger on the strength of a promise, and the fastest way to earn that file is to show the analysis already done on a business they can look up themselves.

1.2

A worked example MUST NOT be built from client data. Not anonymised, not aggregated, not "close enough to be unrecognisable". Client data arrives under an engagement letter that says what it is for, and a marketing example is not what it is for. This holds even where the client would probably say yes: probably is not consent, and consent obtained after the work is done is not a defence.

1.3

A public filing solves the problem completely. The company has already published the numbers, the publication is mandatory rather than negotiated, and the reader can open the same document and check our arithmetic. That last part is the point. An example nobody can audit is a claim; an example anyone can audit is evidence.

1.4

The published examples are illustrative. They demonstrate the analysis, not a result we produced for a paying client, and they are never described as one. Section 8 is not optional and it is not legal throat-clearing: presenting an illustration as a client outcome is a deceptive advertising problem before it is anything else.

Two lists, deliberately The client-facing data request lives in the intake and adapts to what the client engaged, how they are taxed, whether their accounts carry an assurance report and whether they hold inventory. This document is the other list. It assumes no cooperation, no access and no relationship, and it asks what can be assembled from documents the subject was legally required to publish. Neither list is a subset of the other, and the gap between them is the honest limit of what an example can show (§7).

Section 2

What counts as a source

2.1

Admissible. A document filed by the company itself with a securities regulator or a companies registry, or published by the company as its own report to shareholders. In the United States that is the annual report on Form 10-K, the quarterly 10-Q, the current report 8-K, the proxy statement DEF 14A, and the registration statement S-1 or S-4. Outside the United States: the annual report and accounts filed with the relevant registry, the Canadian equivalents on SEDAR+, and UK filings at Companies House.

2.2

Also admissible, with care. The company's own earnings release and investor presentation, filed as an 8-K exhibit. These are useful because they carry segment and unit detail the statements omit, and they are dangerous because they carry non-GAAP measures the company defined itself. Use them for operating detail. Do not use them for headline profitability without reconciling to the audited statements.

2.3

Not admissible as a primary source. Analyst notes, aggregator sites, screener databases, press coverage, and any figure whose provenance is a chart rather than a statement. These may be used to find a company or to sanity-check a number. They may never be the citation. If the only place a figure appears is a third party's summary of a filing, we do not have the figure; we have somebody's transcription of it.

2.4

Every figure carries its citation. Form, filer, fiscal year, and the statement or note it came from. The working file records this as the number is entered, not afterwards from memory. Reconstructing citations at the end is how a wrong number survives into publication.

Choosing the subject Pick a company whose economics resemble an owner-run business: one line of business, one operating model, few segments, a balance sheet a reader can hold in their head. A conglomerate is a poor teaching example because the consolidated statements hide exactly what the Method exists to find. Smaller listed companies and recent S-1 filers are usually the best material, and an S-1 in particular carries several years of statements plus a management discussion written to explain the business to someone who has never seen it.

Section 3

Retrieving the filing

3.1

SEC filings come from EDGAR. Three routes, in order of preference. (a) The company's filing index page, reached from its CIK, which gives the documents as filed. (b) The structured company facts endpoint, which returns every XBRL fact the company has tagged, by concept and period. (c) Full-text search, for finding which filing discusses a topic.

3.2

Prefer the XBRL data over the rendered page. The tagged facts are the same numbers the statements print, already carrying period, unit and scale, which removes the two errors that account for most mistakes in this work: a misread thousand and a misassigned period.

3.3

Keep the source document. The filing is saved into the working folder alongside the analysis, with its accession number in the filename. A live URL is not a record. Filings are amended, indexes are reorganised, and an example whose source cannot be reopened in two years is an example that quietly becomes unverifiable.

3.4

Requests to a regulator's servers identify themselves properly and stay inside the published rate limits. This is a condition of access, not a suggestion, and it is trivially easy to comply with.

Section 4

The pull checklist

Worked in order. Items tagged required are what the workbench needs to run at all; an example missing one of them is not an example, it is an estimate. Items tagged supporting are what turn a set of ratios into a reading of the business.

The three statements

  1. Income statement, three full years required A 10-K carries three years for most filers and two for a smaller reporting company. Where only two are presented, take the third from the prior year's 10-K rather than leaving the trend to two points. Two points make a line; three make a direction.
  2. Balance sheet, four period ends required A 10-K prints two. Take the other two from the prior 10-K. Four dates because every average-balance measure, days sales outstanding, inventory days, return on assets, needs an opening balance as well as a closing one: four balance sheets give three years of ratios, three give two.
  3. Cash flow statement, three full years required Carries depreciation, capital spending, the movement in working capital and everything returned to shareholders. Without it, EBITDA is estimated rather than derived and cash conversion cannot be measured at all.
  4. The auditor's report required Who signed it, what opinion was given, how long they have served, and any critical audit matter or going-concern paragraph. A critical audit matter is the auditor naming the number they found hardest to be sure of, which is almost always the number worth looking at first.

The notes that carry the analysis

  1. Significant accounting policies required Revenue recognition, inventory method, useful lives, capitalisation thresholds. These decide whether two companies' margins can be compared at all, and they are the first thing to check before calling one of them better run.
  2. Segment note required Revenue and operating profit by segment and by geography, plus the basis on which the chief operating decision maker reviews them. This is the closest a filing comes to profitability by line of business, and the segment definition itself tells you how management actually thinks about the company.
  3. Revenue disaggregation required Revenue split by product, service, channel, timing of transfer or contract type. It is the public analogue of the revenue-by-product-or-service file we ask a client for, and it is where mix effects become visible.
  4. Property, plant and equipment, with capital expenditure required Gross cost, accumulated depreciation, additions and disposals, read alongside capex in the cash flow statement. Separates what was actually spent from what was charged, which is what makes capital intensity and maintenance spending visible.
  5. Debt note required Instrument, balance, rate, maturity schedule, covenants and headroom. A covenant is a constraint written down by someone with the power to enforce it, and it belongs in the constraint read whether or not management mentions it.
  6. Lease note required Operating and finance leases, right-of-use assets, and the maturity table. A long lease behaves like borrowing whether or not the reader thinks of it that way, so it sits beside debt, not below it.
  7. Inventory note supporting Only where inventory is held. Raw, work in progress and finished goods, the valuation method, and any reserve. Inventory is the balance-sheet line most often carried at a number nobody has tested, and it moves both margin and working capital.
  8. Receivables, allowance and customer concentration supporting Tests the collection days the statements imply. The concentration disclosure, any customer above ten per cent of revenue, is the public version of the top-ten-customers question, and it is priced by every lender and every buyer.
  9. Deferred revenue and remaining performance obligations supporting Money taken for work not yet done is a liability, not revenue, and the backlog disclosure beside it is the best forward indicator a filing contains.
  10. Related-party transactions supporting Property leased from an entity an insider owns, loans in either direction, family on the payroll. Not wrong and not hidden; it simply has to be normalised out before earnings mean anything.
  11. Commitments, contingencies and guarantees supporting Purchase commitments, litigation, guarantees. Obligations that are real, dated, and absent from the balance sheet.
  12. Subsequent events supporting The statements stop at a date; the business did not. An acquisition, a refinancing or a lost contract after the period end changes what the analysis is allowed to conclude.

What management says about it

  1. Management's discussion and analysis required Management's own explanation of the movement, in their words. Read it before building the model and again after. What they choose to explain, and what they leave to the reader to notice, is itself a finding.
  2. Risk factors, and the business description supporting The company's own statement of what could go wrong, plus the description of the operating model in Item 1. Together they populate several of the seven lenses without a single interview.
  3. Human capital disclosure and headcount supporting The public stand-in for payroll by role. Thin by comparison, but enough for revenue per employee and a rough capacity read.
  4. Proxy statement: compensation and ownership supporting Executive pay, the incentive plan and its metrics, and who holds the shares. The incentive plan tells you what the organisation is actually being paid to maximise, which frequently is not what the strategy section claims.
  5. Non-GAAP reconciliation from the earnings release supporting Read as a source of add-backs, and read sceptically. The reconciliation is the company's own list of what it considers not to count, and reasonable people disagree with parts of it. Where we accept an add-back, we say so; where we reject one, we say that too.
One filing is rarely enough Three years of statements plus four balance sheet dates means two consecutive 10-K filings as a minimum, and often three. Pull them together at the start. Discovering on day two that the fourth balance sheet is missing means redoing the normalisation, not adding to it.

Section 6

Normalising before anything is entered

6.1

Scale. Establish once, at the top of the working file, whether the statements are in units, thousands or millions, and whether the notes use the same scale as the face of the statements. They frequently do not. Every number is entered in units.

6.2

Period. Record the fiscal year end and whether the company uses a 52 or 53-week year. A 53-week year adds roughly two per cent to revenue and to most cost lines, and reading that as growth is an error the company itself will have explained in the MD&A.

6.3

Restatement. Where a prior year is presented differently in the later filing than it was in the earlier one, take the later presentation and note that it was restated. Never mix the two in one trend line. This is the single most common way a public-filing example produces a movement that never happened.

6.4

Discontinued operations. Once an operation is classified as discontinued, prior periods are recast to exclude it from continuing operations while the balance sheet often is not. Decide explicitly which basis the example uses, apply it to every year, and say so in the example.

6.5

Acquisitions. A year containing an acquisition is not comparable to the year before it. Note the closing date and whether the growth being read is organic or bought. Where the filing gives pro forma figures, use them for the comparison and the reported figures for everything else.

6.6

Reconcile before analysing. The balance sheet balances, the cash flow reconciles to the movement in cash, and net income agrees across all three statements. No analysis begins until all three tie. If they do not tie, the transcription is wrong: the filing was audited and the spreadsheet was not.

The one-page provenance record Every example carries a working sheet listing filer, CIK or registry number, accession number, form type, fiscal years used, scale, restatement decisions and the basis chosen for discontinued operations. It takes ten minutes and it is what makes the example defensible a year later when somebody asks where a number came from.

Section 7

What a filing will not give you

7.1

The gap between this list and the client data request is not a shortcoming of the method. It is the honest boundary of what public disclosure contains, and stating it plainly is more persuasive than pretending it is not there.

Asked of a clientIn a public filing
Transaction-level exportNever. Repeat rate, lifetime value and cohort behaviour cannot be computed from any filing.
Customer list with addressesNever. No trade area, no catchment, no penetration mapping.
Payroll or headcount by roleTotal headcount at best. No role structure, no capacity ceiling, no key-person exposure.
Owner compensation and add-backsNot applicable. Executive pay is disclosed; discretionary owner spending does not exist in this form.
Receivables and payables agingBalances only, no aging buckets.
Price list, discounts and packagesRarely, and never completely. Price sensitivity has to be modelled rather than observed.
Revenue by locationRegion or country only. Site-level comparison is impossible.
InterviewsNone. Everything about why is inferred from documents written for a different purpose.
Read-only system accessNone, so no live scoreboard and no continuous layer.
Say this out loud in the example A public-filing example can demonstrate the diagnostic, the financial analysis and the constraint read. It cannot demonstrate customer economics, geographic intelligence or the continuous layer, because the inputs those phases need are not published by anybody. Naming that limit inside the example is not a weakness. It is the clearest possible demonstration of why the client data request asks for what it asks for.

Section 8

Labelling, and what may never be said

8.1

Every published example carries a visible label stating that it is an illustrative analysis built from public filings, that the company is not and has not been a client, and that nothing in it was prepared for or reviewed by the company. The label appears on the artifact itself, not only on the page linking to it.

8.2

Never claim or imply a relationship. No "our work with", no "we helped", no before-and-after framed as a result we produced, no testimonial, no logo, no colour palette borrowed from the subject's brand. The company's name is used descriptively, as the subject of an analysis of its own published accounts, and in no other way.

8.3

Separate observation from recommendation. An observation drawn from the filing is supported by the filing. A recommendation is our judgment, offered on incomplete information, and is labelled as such. A reader must never have to guess which one they are looking at.

8.4

No forward-looking claim about the subject. No target price, no forecast, no assessment of the security, and nothing that reads as investment advice. The example is about method, not about whether anyone should own the shares.

8.5

Correct on contact. If the subject company objects to anything factual, verify it against the filing and correct it the same day if they are right. Being right about a number is worth more than being first to publish it.

8.6

Prefer a subject that is not a prospect. Building a public example of a company we are actively pitching creates a conflict that is hard to explain afterwards. Where it is unavoidable, disclose it in the example.

Section 9

Sign-off before an example is published

  1. All three statements tieBalance sheet balances, cash flow reconciles to the cash movement, net income agrees across the statements, for every year presented.
  2. Every figure traces to a citationForm, filer, accession number, fiscal year, statement or note. Spot-check five at random against the saved filing.
  3. Source documents savedIn the working folder, named by accession number, not linked to a live URL.
  4. Provenance sheet completeScale, fiscal year convention, restatements, discontinued operations basis, acquisitions.
  5. Restatements and 53-week years handledConsistently across the whole trend, and noted in the example.
  6. Limits stated inside the exampleWhat this analysis can and cannot show, per §7, in the example itself rather than in a footnote nobody reads.
  7. Illustrative label present on the artifactPer §8.1, on the document, not only on the page that links to it.
  8. No claimed relationship anywhereCopy, headings, captions, file names, metadata and social preview text all checked.
  9. Observation and recommendation visually separatedA reader can tell which is which without being told.
  10. Read once more as an outsiderSpecifically: would someone who has never met us mistake this for a client engagement? If there is any doubt, the label is not strong enough.
Standard of proof The test for publishing an example is not whether it is impressive. It is whether a sceptical reader holding the same filing could reproduce every number in it. Anything that fails that test comes out, however good it looks.
