Section 1

Purpose, scope and authority

1.1

This document defines the architecture of The Aperture Method™: its objects, phases, contracts, gates and evidence standard. Its purpose is to give every future addition a defined place, so that the Method grows by extension rather than by accretion.

1.2

It governs the SOPs, the agent definitions, the intake instruments, the scoring rubric, the deliverable set, the commercial documents and the codebase. It does not govern commercial terms, pricing or legal drafting.

1.3

Precedence. Where artifacts conflict, authority runs in this order: (a) this document; (b) the Method SOPs; (c) the Agent Build Spec; (d) marketing and commercial material. A conflict resolved here MUST be corrected at source in the losing artifact; this document is not a place to hide a contradiction.

1.4

Normative language. MUST is a requirement; non-conformance is a defect. SHOULD is a strong default; departure must be recorded with a reason. MAY is discretionary.

1.5

The companion Master Process Architecture is descriptive — it shows the system as built at a point in time. This document is normative — it states what the system must be. Where the map shows something this document forbids, the map is reporting a defect, not granting permission.

The governing principle Human-led, AI-enabled. Automation compresses the mechanical work — reading, reconciling, modelling, mapping, monitoring. Judgment, context and accountability remain with a named person. Agents produce findings; only a person produces conclusions. No clause in this document may be read to permit otherwise.

2.3 The people boundary

Leadership & Team is a lens, and key-person risk is the single most common reason a business of this size cannot be sold. That makes people a legitimate object of assessment — and makes the limit of that assessment worth stating precisely, because the failure mode is expensive in two directions: liability, and the loss of the evidence standard everything else rests on.

2.3.1

The Method assesses the system that holds capability, never the individuals in it. In scope: which roles only one person can perform, what revenue depends on them, who could cover, what is documented, how decisions are made, how the structure is shaped, and whether licensed or qualified headcount caps billable volume. These are structural facts about a business.

2.3.2

Out of scope, and MUST be declined: appraisal of named individuals, compensation benchmarking, hiring and firing recommendations, disciplinary or termination advice, worker classification, culture and engagement surveys, and anything else that is properly HR or employment law. Aperture is not licensed to give it, and the exclusion MUST appear in the SOW and in Exhibit A of the engagement contract.

2.3.3

The read-aloud test. A people finding may enter a deliverable only if it would survive being read aloud, unedited, to the person it concerns. “One person can quote work, and $1.4M of revenue depends on it” passes. “This person is not strong enough for the role” does not. The first is a fact about the business; the second is an opinion about a human being, and it cannot be traced to a source under §7.

2.3.4

Quantify the exposure. Where a single-person dependency is found, the deliverable MUST state the revenue or margin at risk over a defined absence — ninety days is the standard window — rather than describing the dependency in adjectives. Rubric criterion ldr-01 scores 80 only where this has been done.

2.3.5

Agents are bound by §4.3 here more tightly than anywhere else: an agent may surface a structural dependency, and MUST NOT characterise a person. No client contact, no conclusions about individuals. An AI system rendering judgment on named employees is the fastest way to lose a client’s trust in everything else the engagement produced.

Section 3

Object taxonomy

Everything in the Method is exactly one of six object types. An artifact that cannot be typed has not been designed yet.

TypeDefinitionMust declare
InputSomething the client supplies: a document, a data export, system access, or a person's time.Which phase needs it · blocking or helpful · the intake question that collects it
MethodAn analytical technique applied to inputs: a calculation, model, rubric or framework.Discipline · phase · inputs consumed · output produced · the evidence class it yields
AgentAn automated worker executing a defined SOP within a fixed tool allowlist.Phase · tool allowlist · refusal rules · which steps are human
OutputAn internal artifact: a score, a finding, a dataset, a model. Not sent to a client.Producing tool · evidence refs · consumers
DeliverableAn artifact the client receives and keeps.Phase · format · the gate it must pass · the pre-delivery check
GateA point where work stops until a named human decides.Who decides · what evidence they see · what the two outcomes are
3.1

Every Method MUST declare the inputs it consumes, and every declared input MUST have a collection route — an intake question, a data request item, or a system connection. A method with no collection route is not yet part of the architecture.

3.2

Every Input MUST have at least one consumer. An input collected but consumed by nothing is a burden placed on the client for no return and SHOULD be removed or wired.

3.3

Every Agent tool allowlist is closed. A capability named in an agent's description but absent from its allowlist is not callable and MUST NOT be advertised as available.

3.4

Every Deliverable MUST pass the pre-delivery check before release, including the internal-identity scrub of §2.1.3.

Section 4

The Force5 agent layer

Force5 is the internal name for the five specialist agents that execute the SOPs. They are a labour layer, not a decision layer. This section is their constitution.

4.1 What the agent layer is

4.1.1

Each agent owns exactly one phase, holds one standard of evidence, and hands typed findings to the next. The Method executes as a sequence, not a swarm.

4.1.2

Agents compress the mechanical work — reading, reconciling, modelling, mapping, monitoring. They MUST NOT close the loop themselves. Every phase ends at a human gate (§8).

4.1.3

Agent identities are internal (§2.1.3). Clients engage the Aperture modules. No agent name, and no reference to "Force5", may appear in a client-facing artifact.

4.3 The agent constitution

These rules bind every agent. They are not style guidance; they are the terms on which automation is permitted to touch client work at all.

4.3.1

Closed allowlist. Each agent may call only the tools on its list. A capability named in an agent's description but absent from its allowlist is not callable and MUST NOT be advertised.

4.3.2

No gate may be closed by an agent. Where a step is hybrid, the agent proposes and a person disposes.

4.3.3

No client contact. Agents do not send, present, or correspond. Every readout is delivered by a person.

4.3.4

Findings, never conclusions. An agent surfaces what the evidence shows. What it means, and what to do, is a human output.

4.3.5

Refusal is correct behaviour. An agent that cannot meet its evidence standard MUST stop and say so. A blocked state is a legitimate terminal state; a plausible answer produced without evidence is a defect, not a service.

4.3.6

No rubric authorship. Agents read the rubric, the weights and the criteria. They MUST NOT invent, adjust or extend them (§10).

4.3.7

Provenance. Every figure an agent emits carries its evidence references and the tool that produced it (§8).

Section 5

The engagement spine

5.1

The canonical sequence is: Commercial acceptance → Intake → Data collection → Ledger → Business X-Ray → Phase 01 → Phases 02 and 03 → Phase 04 → Phase 05 → loop.

5.2

Phases 02 and 03 MAY run concurrently. Both consume Phase 01's typed questions and neither depends on the other.

5.3

Phase 04 MUST NOT begin until at least one of Phases 02 or 03 has been gated. It scores what those phases produce; with neither, it has nothing to score.

5.4

Phase 05 MUST NOT begin without an approved Focus Plan and agreed success measures. A Scoreboard built without them measures activity rather than strategy.

5.5

Standalone purchase. Any module MAY be sold alone. When a module runs without its normal upstream input, it MUST declare the substitution on the face of the deliverable — for example: "Derived from financials — not owner-validated." Silent degradation is prohibited.

5.6

Every phase ends at a gate (§8). A phase MUST NOT begin on ungated inputs.

Section 7

The evidence standard

7.1

Every figure in every deliverable MUST carry an evidence reference resolving to a ledger entry.

7.2

Every figure MUST be produced by a tool that can be re-run. A number produced by reasoning is not evidence and MUST NOT be published.

7.3

Every value carries exactly one class:

ClassMeaningMay support a score
MeasuredFrom a client system or documentYes
DerivedComputed by a named tool from measured inputsYes
AssumedA judgment input; cites who set it and on what basisOnly where the rubric permits
ClaimedAsserted in interview, not yet corroboratedNo, until promoted
7.4

Reconciliation. Two thresholds exist and MUST NOT be confused. Ledger acceptance tolerates aggregate variance of ≤0.5% provided every variance is itemised — it is a completeness threshold. Figure publication tolerates nothing: any figure entering a deliverable must reconcile exactly to its source. Accepting a ledger does not license an unreconciled figure.

7.5

Insufficient evidence is a finding. A lens, criterion or figure that cannot be evidenced MUST be marked insufficient and MUST NOT be estimated. Insufficient is never scored as zero, and its weight is never redistributed.

7.6

Ledger references are append-only. A superseded entry is retained and marked; a restatement by the client is itself a finding.

7.7

Confidence is recorded separately from score. A well-evidenced 40 and a thinly-evidenced 40 are different findings.

Section 8

The gate register

"Seven gates" was referenced across the source material but never enumerated. This is the enumeration. A gate is blocking unless marked advisory.

GateNameDecided byPasses when
G1Scope acceptedFenwick + clientScope, exclusions, fee and timeline are signed. Exclusions are recorded explicitly.
G2Ledger acceptedFenwickSources ingested, reconciled within tolerance, every variance itemised, gaps recorded. Nothing downstream runs until this passes.
G3Constraint namedFenwick, then ownerOne constraint named from ranked candidates, agreed with the owner in writing.
G4Basis approvedFenwickPhase-specific judgment inputs approved before computation: allocation basis (02), trade-area parameters and scoring criteria (03), matrix criteria and weights (04), KPI definitions and thresholds (05).
G5Phase output acceptedFenwickGuarantees in the phase contract met; recomputation and reconciliation passed; insufficiencies marked.

8.1

Each gate MUST produce a record: who decided, when, on what evidence, and the reasoning. On a retainer, the reasoning is what makes the Scoreboard interpretable a year later.

8.2

A blocked state is a legitimate terminal state. Partial artifacts MUST NOT be released to work around a gate.

8.3

Agents MUST NOT close a gate. Where a step is marked hybrid, the agent produces a proposal and a person disposes of it.

Section 9

Load-bearing objects

Four objects are consumed by three or more downstream components. If one is wrong, everything after it is wrong and nothing will say so. Each requires a defined home, not a mention in prose.

9.1

A load-bearing object MUST NOT exist only inside a document. If it cannot be read by a tool, it cannot be enforced, and the guarantees in §6 and §7 become aspirations.

Section 10

Scoring authority

10.1

The rubric — lenses, sub-criteria, anchors and weights — is the practice's intellectual property. Agents read it; they MUST NOT invent, adjust or extend it.

10.2

An unratified rubric MUST NOT produce a client-facing Aperture Score. This is enforced in code, not by discipline.

10.3

Sub-criteria are scored 0–100 against anchor descriptions at 20, 50 and 80. A lens score is the mean of its scored sub-criteria. The Aperture Score is the weighted mean of lens scores.

10.4

Below 70% coverage no composite is published. The deliverable in that case is a data request.

10.5

Comparability. Re-scoring MUST use the stored rubric version and weights, never the current ones. Where the rubric changes, history is either restated explicitly or labelled non-comparable. Silent mixing of versions is prohibited — it destroys the only thing a baseline is for.

10.6

Rubric changes are versioned semantically: a weight or lens change is a major version; a sub-criterion or anchor rewording is a minor version.

Section 11

Placement rules

This is the operative section. Anything new — a form, an agent, an assessment, a model, a deliverable, an MBA-level method — is placed by answering these questions in order. If any answer is unavailable, the thing is not ready to be built.

10.1 The placement procedure

1

What type is it? One of the six in §3. If it is two things, it is two objects and must be split.

2

Which phase owns it? Exactly one phase owns it. Others may consume it. If no phase owns it, it belongs to a cross-cutting layer and must be declared as such.

3

What does it consume? Every input named, and for each: the collection route that already exists, or the new intake question required. A method whose inputs are not collected is not yet buildable.

4

What does it produce? The output object, its evidence class, and every downstream consumer. An output with no consumer is not an output.

5

Which gate does it pass? Name the gate from §7 and who decides. If it requires a judgment input, that input passes G4 before computation.

6

What does it displace? Does it duplicate or supersede something? Retire the predecessor in the same change; do not leave both.

7

How is it evidenced? The tool that produces it, and how a hostile reader would trace its figures to source.

8

Register it. Amend this document and the Master Process Architecture in the same change.

10.2 Placing a new analytical method

The Method's differentiation rests on applying graduate-level quantitative work to businesses that cannot otherwise buy it. That claim is only defensible where the work is actually done.

11.2.1

A method MUST declare: its discipline; the phase that owns it; the inputs it requires; the output it produces; the decision it informs; and the evidence class of that output.

11.2.2

A method MUST NOT be advertised as applied until it has appeared in a delivered client artifact with its working shown. Until then it is catalogued, not applied, and MUST be labelled as such in internal material.

11.2.3

A method MUST NOT be included whose inputs the intake does not collect. Adding the method and the question is one change, not two.

11.2.4

Sophistication is not a justification. A method earns its place by changing a decision the owner would otherwise make differently — not by being advanced.

10.3 Placing a new client input

11.3.1

Every new question MUST name its consumer — a rubric sub-criterion, an SOP step, or a named method — before it is added to any instrument.

11.3.2

Every input is classified blocking or helpful, and carries a plain-language reason the client can read.

11.3.3

References between instruments and the rubric MUST use stable identifiers, never positional numbering. Positional references break silently when a question is inserted.

10.4 Placing a new deliverable

11.4.1

A deliverable MUST name the phase, the executive question it answers, the gate it passes, and the format the client keeps.

11.4.2

If it cannot be traced to an existing phase contract's guarantee, either the contract is amended or the deliverable is not in scope.

10.5 Placing a new agent or tool

11.5.1

A tool MUST be added to exactly one agent's allowlist, with a documented contract and its refusal conditions.

11.5.2

An agent MUST NOT gain a capability that closes a gate, contacts a client, or states a conclusion.

Section 12

Change control

12.1

This document is amended by Fenwick How. No other artifact may contradict it; a contradiction is a defect in that artifact.

12.2

Versioning: major for a change to phases, object types, gates or the evidence standard; minor for a phase contract, placement rule or vocabulary change; patch for clarification without behavioural effect.

12.3

Every amendment MUST record what changed, why, and which artifacts must be updated to conform.

12.4

An amendment affecting the rubric, the evidence standard or a gate MUST state its effect on engagements already in flight, including whether prior scores remain comparable.

12.5

The Master Process Architecture SHOULD be regenerated with each amendment so the descriptive and normative views do not drift.
